# Issue registry

The single record of defects and design findings in `synpmx`. It replaces
`design/REVIEW_BACKLOG.md` and `design/TEST_SIM.md`, which were merged on
2026-08-14: they had the same schema and the split between them ("mechanism,
privacy and API" against "simulation defects and their gates") had broken down
in both directions.

Two ID prefixes survive, as history rather than as a classification. **Never
reuse or renumber an ID**: `R/`, `tests/testthat/`, `scripts/` and several
shipped vignettes cite them directly, and a citation that resolves to a
different defect is worse than one that resolves to nothing.

- `REV-###` — found by reading code.
- `SIM-###` — found by running the generator and looking at its output.

Open work is below, grouped by area. Everything closed is a compressed
regression record further down: what broke, why, and the check that keeps it
from coming back. A closed row keeps its narrative only where that narrative
records a **decision or a rejected alternative** somebody would otherwise
retry.

To add an issue: write the row before changing any code, add the smallest
deterministic failing test, then fix. Do not close on visual inspection alone.
The testing and documentation workflow around this file lives in `AGENTS.md`;
it is not repeated here.

## Open — differential privacy: mechanism and accounting

| ID | Issue | Direction |
|---|---|---|
| `REV-002` | Nothing warns a user that their N is too small for their epsilon and release dimension until the budget is irreversibly spent. The existing check (`R/fit.R:252`) fires on `dimensions > 6 * count` and ignores epsilon entirely. | **Partly delivered.** `pmx_preflight()` (`R/calibrate.R:90`) reports `f = d / (epsilon * N)` and the resulting fold-error, consuming no budget and reading no data — which is what this asked for, for the calibrated path. Still open: the six-group `synpmx_empirical()` release has no equivalent, and the `N >~ sensitivity / (epsilon_group * target_error)` envelope is documented in `articles/feasibility.Rmd` rather than reported at the call site. |
| `REV-003` | Data-dependent `stop()` on confidential rows *before* any noise is applied: `validate_pmx(..., strict = TRUE)` at `R/fit.R:206`, plus `R/representation.R:90` and `R/representation.R:215`. Whether the call throws — and its message — is a function of one individual's record, which is an unaccounted output channel in exactly the place the package claims formal DP. | Drop or clip out-of-domain records rather than erroring, matching how numeric bounds are already handled by `.clip()`. If a hard failure must stay, route it through a private test and account for it. Either way disclose it in `proof_assumptions` (`R/fit.R:271`) and `articles/privacy-argument.Rmd`. |
| `REV-004` | `delta` is validated and requires a written justification when positive (`R/fit.R:14-24`), but every mechanism is Laplace and every accounting entry hardcodes `delta = 0` (`R/privacy.R:158`). Users justify a parameter that is never spent. | Either restrict the API to `delta = 0` and say so, or wire it to a real approximate-DP mechanism — the same work as `REV-006`. Do not leave it decorative. |
| `REV-005` | `sensitivity = ncol(matrix)` is sound but pessimistic. A subject occupies a handful of trajectory/timing cells, not all of them; the true row L1 is bounded by `2 * min(cells, observation_limit)` per endpoint, and the timing matrix's true bound is `n_cells + 2 * max_occasions`. | Derive the bound from the contribution limits already declared in `pmx_contribution_limits()` rather than from matrix width. Cheap, no change to the proof structure, and it makes `max_timing_cells` do real work — today it only caps grid size (`R/endpoints.R:157, 182`). |
| `REV-006` | Pure-DP Laplace under basic sequential composition costs linearly in dimension. Gaussian under zCDP/RDP costs roughly `sqrt(d)` and composes better across the six query groups. | Add a zCDP accountant alongside the basic-composition one and a Gaussian measurement in the OpenDP adapter; keep pure DP the default. The natural consumer of `REV-004`'s `delta`. **Note the caveat in `articles/feasibility.Rmd:591`** — the advice is dimension dependent and Gaussian only wins once `d` is large enough. |
| `REV-007` | The trajectory group spends 40 numbers on per-cell presence/value pairs to describe what is qualitatively a 3-4 parameter curve. Dimension is the dominant cost term under any accountant. | Investigate releasing a low-dimensional shape (spline or basis coefficients, or a few quantiles) instead of a dense grid. Highest ceiling on this list, and the most work. |
| `REV-008` | The decoders carry unlabelled rescue logic: `.ensure_grid_presence()` forces at least 3 cells regardless of the release (`R/generate.R:119`), variance floors `0.05^2`/`0.01^2`/`0.25` (`R/representation.R:658-660`), the `0.95` rate cap (`:686`). All are privacy-safe post-processing, but collectively they mean that at high noise the generator is mostly sampling hardcoded priors — which undercuts "source-calibrated". | Promote them to named documented constants, and add a diagnostic reporting what fraction of the output shape came from the release versus the fallbacks, so the claim is measurable rather than asserted. |
| `REV-012` | `run_dp_backend_tests()` advertises a `"privacy_map"` entry (`R/privacy.R:135`) but `passed` only checks lengths and finiteness. The map check happens incidentally inside `release()`. | Assert the privacy relation explicitly, or stop naming a test that is not run. |
| `REV-013` | `.opendp_backend()` calls `enable_features("contrib")` on every resolve (`R/privacy.R:5`), mutating global OpenDP state as a side effect of ordinary package use. | Do it once, guarded, and document it. |
| `REV-016` | The declared adjacency is "add-or-remove one complete subject" and `.bound_subject_contributions()` groups rows by the ID column, which is correct only if one person appears exactly once. Rollover and extension studies, crossovers pooled with parallel-group studies, and re-enrolled subjects all violate it: a person contributing `k` records receives roughly `k * epsilon` by group privacy, and nothing in the accounting, the ledger, or `validate_private_model()` reveals it. Matters more now that pooling is a recommended path. | Require an explicit assertion that IDs are unique persons, or accept a person-level grouping column and bound contributions on that. Stated for readers at `articles/data-elicitation.Rmd:103,207`. |
| `REV-017` | Realized trial-design quantities (dose levels reached, cohort sizes after expansion) are formally functions of the source dataset, so treating them as fixed public inputs is an assumption rather than a derivation. Practically low risk — such facts are usually already published, the inference from a stopping point to a cause is many-to-one and confounded, and a committee's decision about a program is not one individual's record. | Record realized design in `proof_assumptions` with a source field, so a reviewer can see it. Separately, enforce that per-subject escalation and titration sequences are generated from the public rule and never copied from source subjects. See `articles/data-elicitation.Rmd:192`. |

### Differential privacy and decoding

| ID | Area | What broke, and why | Pinned by |
|---|---|---|---|
| `REV-001` / `SIM-020` | Decoding | Decoders compared released presence fields against the bare constant `0.25` as if they were probabilities, but those fields are unnormalized subject counts of order N. **Do not add all twelve ACOP sets.** They differ in the PK *model* used to
simulate them, which `synpmx` never sees — it reads structure, not
pharmacology. In shape they reduce to four: bolus, infusion (adds `RATE`),
oral, and with or without `SS`/`ADDL`/`II`. One or two representatives is the
whole value.

Not PMX-shaped and not usable here: `invgaussian`, `metabolite`, `pump`,
`rats` and `Wang2007` (no `EVID`/`AMT`, and mostly under 40 rows), and
`xgxr::mad_nca` (NCA-derived summaries rather than an event table).

Nothing outside these two packages has been surveyed. `PKPDdatasets`, the
pharmaverse SDTM/ADaM sets and `mrgsolve`'s examples are plausible sources of
ADaM-shaped data and are not installed here; adding a dependency for test data
should clear a higher bar than adding a dataset from a package already in
`Suggests`.

Public datasets must be loaded from their installed packages during a run. Do
not copy their records into this repository, and use only public or
package-generated data in committed tests, reports and examples.

Shapes still unrepresented, for when there is a reason to add one: IV bolus
decay, irregular dosing, below-quantification patterns, missing covariates,
and genuinely multi-phasic dose-relative profiles.
